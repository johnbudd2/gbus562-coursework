# Compliant physician query rules

These rules are written in this project's own words. They are based on the
principles in the AHIMA/ACDIS *Guidelines for Achieving a Compliant Query
Practice* (2022 update) and the ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines for Coding and
Reporting (FY 2027). They are a summary for this course project, not a copy
of either document.

## When to query

- When the record has clinical indicators of a condition that the provider has
  not documented.
- When documentation is incomplete (for example, a condition without its type or
  acuity), ambiguous, or conflicting.
- Only the patient's provider can document a diagnosis. Findings recorded by
  other clinicians, such as a dietitian, can support a query but cannot be coded
  as the diagnosis (Guideline I.B.14).
- Do not query for an abnormal finding that has no clinical significance, such
  as a single lab value that was not evaluated, treated, or monitored
  (Section III.B).

## What every query must contain

1. **Clinical indicators taken from the record.** These can be lab values, vital
   signs, exam findings, imaging results, treatments, and monitoring, quoted as
   written. Never add facts that are not in the record.
2. **A neutral question** that asks the provider to clarify, in their own clinical
   judgment.
3. **Answer options** (multiple choice) or a blank (open-ended).

## Multiple-choice queries

- List every clinically reasonable option that the indicators support, not only
  the diagnosis the reviewer suspects. Each option must be clinically plausible
  for this patient.
- Always include **"Other (please specify)"** and **"Clinically undetermined"**.
- Do not include options that the record does not support.
- Do not rank, highlight, or order the options to suggest an answer.

## Yes/no queries

Use these only to confirm present-on-admission status, a cause-and-effect
relationship, or to resolve conflicting documentation. Never use a yes/no
question to ask for a new diagnosis.

## Never in a query (leading or noncompliant)

- Any mention of payment, reimbursement, DRG, CC, MCC, severity level, case mix,
  quality scores, audits, or denials, or any statement of how an answer would
  affect the hospital.
- Telling the provider what to document ("please document…", "please add…",
  "consider documenting…", "to support…").
- Presenting a diagnosis as already established ("the patient has…", "the
  patient meets criteria for…").
- Questions that suggest the answer ("Would you agree that…", "Don't you
  think…", "Can you confirm that…").
- ICD-10-CM codes.
- Questioning or criticizing the provider's clinical judgment.

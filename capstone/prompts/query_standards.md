# Physician Query Standards

A physician query asks the provider to clarify documentation. It must let the provider answer from their own clinical judgment. These standards apply to every query the tool drafts and every query a CDI specialist sends.

## 1. Present the clinical indicators

- Quote the clinical indicators from the medical record, with their **values and dates** (or hospital day). Examples: lab results, vital signs, exam findings, imaging, treatments, and monitoring.
- Use only facts that are in the record. Do not round, summarize, or interpret them.
- Do not state or imply the desired diagnosis. The indicators must speak for themselves.

## 2. Ask, don't suggest

- Ask a neutral question, for example "Based on your clinical judgment, can this condition be further specified?"
- When documentation is **vague or incomplete** (for example "heart failure" without type or acuity) or **conflicting**, ask the provider to clarify it. Do not propose the answer.
- Never tell the provider what to document ("please document…", "consider adding…").
- Never present a diagnosis as established ("the patient has…", "meets criteria for…").
- Never use leading questions ("Would you agree that…?", "Can you confirm that…?").

## 3. One condition per query

- Each query addresses one condition or one documentation question. If a chart raises two issues, send two queries.

## 4. Multiple-choice queries

- List the clinically reasonable options that the indicators support, not only the diagnosis the reviewer suspects.
- Always include **"Other (please specify)"** and **"Unable to determine"**.
- Do not order, highlight, or word the options to favor an answer.
- Yes/no questions may not be used to ask for a new diagnosis.

## 5. Clinical support

- Never introduce a diagnosis, or an answer option, that has no clinical support in the record.
- Do not query an abnormal finding that was not evaluated, treated, or monitored.

## 6. Never mention impact

- No mention of reimbursement, payment, DRG, CC/MCC, severity or risk scores, case mix, quality-score impact, audits, or denials.
- No ICD-10-CM codes in the query.

## 7. Review

- Every query is a draft until a CDI specialist reviews it. The provider's answer, including "Unable to determine", is always acceptable and must be recorded as given.
